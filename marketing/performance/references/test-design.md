# Test Design

## Order of spend

1. People who already know the brand, and the owned list. They cost least and convert
   most. An unanswered hand-raiser is free; contact them before buying strangers.
2. Borrowed audiences with a record of converting (check the company's own paid rate by
   source).
3. Cold audiences, in small amounts, to learn cost.

When ad prices are seasonally high, spend that period on group 1 and build the cold
audience before it.

## Stages

Split the budget. Stage 1 is the smallest amount that can produce a readable early
signal. Later stages are released on a named early result, because the deciding-day
result often lands after the money must be spent. Keep a reserve for what is working.
Write the release rule before launch.

## Messages and ads

- Test messages, not single ads. Group ads by message and read paid results per message.
- Make several different concepts and expect most to lose. Two versions per concept is
  enough to start.
- Each message carries a reason to believe it, taken from the company's own data where
  possible (for example, the content people pay after).
- Check current evidence on creative practice each run and state its strength. Do not
  carry a practice forward from a previous campaign as proven.

## Lines

Set three before launch, per message and per channel:

- **Scale:** at or under the ceiling on the deciding day.
- **Change and retest:** between the ceiling and the stop line. Name the one thing to
  change (ad, page, offer, audience).
- **Stop:** over the stop line, or no plausible fix.

Also name the early signal used for weekly moves (usually cost per signup plus early
payments) and say plainly that it is a proxy.

## Holdout

Use one only where it can be read. Ads aimed at the company's own list are the usual
case: split the list at random, show ads to half, send everyone the same email, and
compare payments on the deciding day. State in advance how big a gap is within chance.
Never compare people who opted in with everyone else; they were already the keenest.

## Feasibility check

For each rule, ask: can it be applied in the time the plan allows? A measure that
matures after the budget must be spent cannot gate that budget. Give it an early proxy
or move the date.

## Offer effects to watch

- A new cheaper plan can move buyers down instead of adding buyers. Track plan mix.
- Discount cohorts may renew worse. Follow them past the first period.
- Count overlapping free offers before launch; several at once blur the read.
