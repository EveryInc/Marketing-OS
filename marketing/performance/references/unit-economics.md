# Unit Economics

A paying customer from an ad is the end of a chain. Find the weakest link and fix it.

## The chain

| Link | Plain meaning | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to be seen (CPM) | Cost for 1,000 people to see the ad | The auction; you choose when and whom |
| Click rate (CTR) | Share of viewers who click | The ad |
| Landing page conversion | Share of clicks who sign up or start checkout | The page and what it asks |
| Paid conversion | Share of signups who pay | Offer, onboarding, email |
| Cost per paying customer (CAC) | Spend divided by people who paid | All four links |
| Payback | First-year value divided by CAC | Price, plan mix, retention |

Many clicks with few payments points at the page or the offer, not the ad.

## The ceiling

Ceiling = first-year value of a new customer ÷ the return the company requires.

- Work it out per plan. A cheap plan has a low ceiling; one blended ceiling hides that.
- State whether the return rule means revenue or profit. If nobody has said, ask.
- State what the cost side includes: ad spend only, or production and staff too.
- Report first-year revenue per unit spent alongside CAC when plans differ in price.

## What a signup can cost

Most you can pay per signup = ceiling × share of signups who pay by the deciding day.

Make this a small table across plausible paid rates before choosing channels. Use the
company's own paid rate by source where it exists. People who arrive from an ad usually
pay less often than people who arrive on their own, so treat organic rates as generous.

## What the budget can buy

Budget ÷ best-case CAC and budget ÷ worst-case CAC give the range. Put that range next
to the goal. When it falls short, name the other sources (the existing list, the offer,
retention) and size each one from the company's own records, or mark it unsized.

## What a small test can and cannot tell you

- Telling two paid rates apart takes thousands of signups per version. A small cell
  compares cost per signup between ads. It cannot compare paid results.
- Paid results come from pooling by message or channel and waiting for the deciding day.
- Platforms need a steady flow of the event they optimize for. When payments are too
  few, optimize for the nearest frequent step and judge on payments later, by cohort.
- Results arrive after the spend. Put the readout date on the calendar before launch.
