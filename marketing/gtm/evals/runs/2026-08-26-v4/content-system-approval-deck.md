# Launch Content System

## A 15-minute leadership approval deck

**Artifact mode:** Content-system approval deck  
**Launch tier:** Standard S/M  
**Review scope:** Launch day and the first three full weeks

---

# Act 1: Decision and Behavior

## Slide 1: The Decision

Approve one launch content system and authorize production for launch day plus the first three full weeks: one hero film, three product demonstrations, three brand-format installments, three proof or education releases, one editorial explainer, and necessary channel adaptations. Leadership makes six open selections; after approval, the team makes, publishes, and ships the system without returning for post-by-post approval.

### Six open selections

1. **Launch date**
2. **Production owner**
3. **On-camera talent**
4. **Second proof case**
5. **Workflow two**
6. **Workflow three**

**What approval unlocks:** Production of the complete mandate, channel-ready adaptations, publishing against the approved cadence, conversion QA, and post-launch measurement.

---

## Slide 2: The Goal

Move the launch audience from awareness to demonstrated understanding, then to one action: **Start with the product.**

- **Behavior change:** People see the product complete meaningful work and start with it.
- **Perception:** The product is understood through finished results, not a list of features.
- **Primary action:** **Start with the product.**
- **CTA rule:** Use the same CTA across the hero asset, recurring formats, editorial, proof, education, and channel adaptations.

---

## Slide 3: The Approach

The launch works as one connected content system:

| System role | Job |
|---|---|
| Hero film | Make the campaign idea and product memorable |
| Product demonstrations | Show one request, the product action, and the finished result |
| Brand formats | Turn the product tension into recognizable, repeatable entertainment |
| Proof | Verify that the shown result happened |
| Education | Teach the audience how to make the behavior useful |
| Editorial | Explain the work pattern and establish authority |
| Channel adaptations | Reframe shared assets for their destination while preserving the proof and CTA |

Each role advances the same audience journey. None becomes a separate campaign.

---

## Slide 4: The Market Jobs

The system must help the audience answer five questions:

1. **What does the product do?** Product demonstrations show complete work.
2. **Did the result happen?** Proof releases supply the receipt.
3. **How should I use it?** Education and editorial explain the behavior.
4. **Why will I remember it?** The hero film and brand format give the launch a repeatable shape.
5. **What do I do next?** Every path ends with **Start with the product.**

---

## Slide 5: The Governing Creative Rule

**Show the work from request to finished result.**

Every asset must contain three beats:

1. A concrete request or recognizable tension.
2. The product or participant taking a visible action.
3. A finished result or changed state the audience can inspect.

If the finished result is missing, the asset is not ready to publish.

# Act 2: Campaign Sequence

## Slide 6: Four Phases, One Causal Backbone

| Phase | Goal | Actions | Proof | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch day: Reveal** | Make the system and product memorable | Release the hero film and editorial explainer | Show the verified homepage-update workflow inside the launch story | **Start with the product.** |
| **Week 1: Make it concrete** | Establish how the product turns a request into finished work | Publish product demonstration one, brand installment one, and proof release one | Request a homepage update; change the approved headline; return the published page | **Start with the product.** |
| **Week 2: Broaden proof** | Show that the system extends beyond the first example | Publish product demonstration two, brand installment two, and proof release two | Workflow two and the second proof case are selected before production | **Start with the product.** |
| **Week 3: Teach transfer** | Help the audience recognize and apply the work pattern | Publish product demonstration three, brand installment three, and one education release | Workflow three supplies the request, action, and finished result used in teaching | **Start with the product.** |

The sequence earns each later claim. It does not schedule reactions or audience participation before the launch gives people something to respond to.

# Act 3

## Content System

**Slide 7: The approved production mandate**

| Content role | Approved volume | Timing |
|---|---:|---|
| Hero launch film | 1 | Launch day |
| Product demonstrations | 3 | Monday in each full week |
| Brand-format installments | 3 | Wednesday in each full week |
| Proof or education releases | 3 | Friday in each full week |
| Editorial explainer | 1 | Launch day |
| Channel adaptations | As necessary | Released only when an adaptation has a distinct audience job |

The Friday allocation resolves into two proof releases and one education release, preserving the approved total of three proof or education releases.

---

## Slide 8: Hero Launch Film

**Premise:** One piece of work moves from a clear request to a finished result. The film expands that pattern across the three approved product workflows.

**Opening example:**

- **Request:** Update the homepage.
- **Action:** Change the approved headline.
- **Result:** Return the published page.

**Film structure:**

1. Establish the approved product tension.
2. Show the verified homepage workflow from request to published page.
3. Show workflows two and three after leadership selects them.
4. End on the completed work and **Start with the product.**

**Production dependency:** The film can be fully scripted after the on-camera talent, workflow two, and workflow three selections are made. No cast member or shooting location is assumed here.

---

## Slide 9: Product-Led Format

**Premise:** One demonstration proves one customer advantage through one complete workflow.

**Repeatable structure:**

- Put the request on screen in the audience's language.
- Show the product taking the action without turning the clip into a feature tour.
- Hold on the finished result long enough to inspect it.
- End with **Start with the product.**

### Three installments

| Demonstration | Request | Product action | Finished result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Homepage update** | Update the homepage | Change the approved headline | Return the published page |
| **2. Workflow two** | **Selection required: Workflow two** | Document the selected product action | Document the selected finished result |
| **3. Workflow three** | **Selection required: Workflow three** | Document the selected product action | Document the selected finished result |

The selected workflows must reach a visible result before production begins.

---

## Slide 10: Brand-Led Format

**Premise:** A recognizable work tension returns in the same creative frame; the product changes the outcome.

- **Tension:** Open on the friction or unfinished task behind one approved workflow.
- **Action:** Dramatize the shift as the product completes the work.
- **Product reveal:** Land on the finished result, then **Start with the product.**
- **Repeatable variation:** Change the workflow and tension while keeping the frame, pacing, and reveal recognizable.

### Three installments

1. **Homepage tension:** The approved headline needs to change; the product updates it and returns the published page.
2. **Workflow two tension:** Build the installment after workflow two is selected and verified.
3. **Workflow three tension:** Build the installment after workflow three is selected and verified.

The brand format dramatizes the tension. The product demonstration explains the workflow. They must not collapse into the same edit.

---

## Slide 11: Proof System

Proof converts a shown workflow into a credible receipt.

### Proof release one: Homepage update

- **Request:** Update the homepage.
- **Action:** Change the approved headline.
- **Result:** Return the published page.
- **Publishing standard:** Show enough of the request, approved change, and published result for an unfamiliar viewer to follow the causal link.

### Proof release two: Second proof case

- **Request:** Defined by the selected second proof case.
- **Action:** Document the verified action taken.
- **Result:** Document the finished result and consequence.
- **Publishing dependency:** Select and verify the second proof case before scripting or scheduling the release.

The proof library verifies outcomes. It does not repeat the hero film or become a general feature reel.

---

## Slide 12: Education Format

**Premise:** Teach the audience how a strong work request becomes an inspectable result.

- Start with the exact request from workflow three once selected.
- Annotate the product action and any decision the audience needs to understand.
- End on the finished result and explain how to inspect it.
- Close with **Start with the product.**

The education release teaches the behavior. It does not introduce a fourth workflow or a second CTA.

---

## Slide 13: Editorial Explainer

**Job:** Establish authority and explain the work pattern behind the launch.

**Article structure:**

1. State the approved product tension.
2. Explain why complete work is the standard for evaluating the product.
3. Use the verified homepage example: request the update, change the approved headline, and return the published page.
4. Show how the recurring content system will keep proving the pattern over the next three weeks.
5. End with **Start with the product.**

The explainer must be useful on its own. It supports the launch rather than transcribing the hero film.

---

# Act 4: Distribution

## Slide 14: Channel Role

- Publish the complete hero film on the launch's approved full-length video destination.
- Use shorter adaptations only when they create a distinct entry point into the hero idea, product workflow, proof, education, or editorial argument.
- Change the copy, opening emphasis, and framing for each destination while preserving the request, action, result, and **Start with the product.**
- Test a native format only when it helps the audience inspect the work more clearly.
- Keep response guidance and post-level copy in the operating plan, outside this approval review.

No new channel destination is assumed by this deck.

# Act 5: Burn-Down and Approval

## Lock

**Slide 17**

Make the same six open selections named at the start of the review:

1. **Launch date**
2. **Production owner**
3. **On-camera talent**
4. **Second proof case**
5. **Workflow two**
6. **Workflow three**

Then lock the script, verified claims, final calendar dates derived from the launch date, and evidence permissions. These are production actions governed by the six selections, not additional leadership selections.

---

## Make

**Slide 18**

- Script and produce the hero film using the three approved workflows.
- Capture three complete product demonstrations.
- Build one recognizable brand-format template and produce three installments.
- Prepare two proof releases and one education release.
- Create front cards, end cards, captions, cutdowns with distinct audience jobs, required ratios, and archived source files.

The selected production owner assigns execution responsibility after approval.

---

## Publish

**Slide 19**

- Edit and publish the editorial explainer.
- Clear the homepage receipt and selected second proof case for publication.
- Write channel-specific copy that preserves the request, action, result, and CTA.
- Release the Monday, Wednesday, and Friday cadence for three full weeks.
- Keep Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, and Sunday blank unless the operating team documents a distinct audience job before adding an adaptation.

---

## Ship

**Slide 20**

- Deploy the approved channel adaptations.
- QA every **Start with the product** path before publication.
- Verify that every proof asset shows the request, action, and finished result.
- Track completed starts as the primary decision metric; use content-role performance as diagnosis.
- Review the system after the third full week and decide which formats continue.

The launch date determines the calendar dates and review date. It does not create another leadership selection.
