# Atlas Social Brief

## Story

Most companies count AI licenses, seats, and installed tools. Those numbers prove procurement. They say little about whether the company works differently.

Every’s position: AI adoption becomes real when it changes decisions and operating behavior. A team has adopted AI when people rewrite workflows, move authority, alter what gets measured, or make choices they could not make before.

Atlas makes behavior the evidence. On Oct. 20, Every launches the Atlas report and an executive roundtable built around that argument. Dan introduces the thesis. Every follows with the report, participant announcements, examples of behavior change, and the roundtable application.

Every social beat should press one question: **What changed after AI arrived?**

## Actions and Behavior

The primary action is to download the Atlas report. The secondary action is to apply to the executive roundtable.

Social should prompt distinct behavior from each audience:

- Executives question adoption dashboards that reward tool installation without showing changes in judgment, speed, authority, or output.
- Operators compare their workflows against the patterns in Atlas and identify where AI has changed the work itself.
- Builders share concrete examples of behavior change: a decision made differently, a workflow rewritten, or a team operating under new rules.

Dan owns the opening argument. Every turns it into a campaign through report findings, participant announcements, practitioner examples, and a direct application CTA. Participants and employees should share the behavior that challenged or confirmed their own view of adoption.

## Ideas to Discuss

- **Bad-dashboard confessions:** Ask leaders and operators to name the adoption metric they no longer trust, then explain what it concealed.
- **Atlas adoption scorecard:** A compact diagnostic that scores behavioral change across decisions, workflows, authority, and measurement. The result should lead into the report.
- **Operator nominations:** Invite the public to nominate operators whose teams work differently because of AI. Feature the strongest examples as proof.
- **Opposing practitioner claims:** Publish two credible, conflicting positions on what counts as adoption. Ask people to choose a side, supply evidence, and see how Atlas resolves or complicates the argument.
- **“What changed?” examples:** Short posts or cards pairing an old operating behavior with the decision or workflow now used. Keep them specific enough for another operator to compare against their own work.
