# Durable Lessons From GTM Edits

These rules come from repeated corrections during the Thesis and Every Agent planning
processes. Apply the durable lessons across launches. Keep the project mechanics as
examples.

## Durable rules

- Interview before drafting when the story, primary action, or authority is unclear.
- Begin with the strategy and intended audience behavior. Add logistics afterward.
- Preserve the user's exact campaign terms and canonical opening.
- Give the campaign a mechanism that can direct many actions.
- Explain the launch through a causal backbone before writing the calendar.
- Keep a master GTM plan and a shorter social brief as separate artifacts.
- Make audience behavior specific. Builders, Operators, Seekers, and Executives may require different movement.
- Verify current names, dates, roles, quotes, approvals, and prices.
- Label derived workback dates as proposals rather than source facts.
- Use an actual contributor statement when claiming an editorial connection. Accept `N/A`.
- Run multiple strategic lanes simultaneously when the idea requires it.
- Use all calendar days in a dated campaign window.
- Keep open questions open.
- Make scoped edits locally and preserve protected sections exactly.
- Keep metrics subordinate to the strategy unless measurement is the assignment.
- Remove generic event language and AI-sounding abstractions.
- Map product truth, strategy, canonical copy, proof, structure, and execution to their
  authoritative sources before synthesizing them.
- Lock the one-line strategy and positioning before building execution.
- Make each sentence and section do one strategic job. Keep product promise, positioning,
  hero UVP, mechanism, reason to believe, and proof distinct.
- Give every strategy slide one job in the argument. If it adds no new idea, decision,
  proof, or action, cut or combine it before polishing the copy.
- Never shrink slide type to force an exhaustive list or dense table onto one page.
  Split the material across additional slides and group it by the decision the audience
  needs to make, so every page remains legible at presentation size.
- Define a channel strategy as a causal theory of audience movement before choosing
  content buckets. Map every bucket to a job in that theory.
- Keep the first champion and the next user distinct when adoption spreads through a
  team. Do not invent skepticism or resistance that the evidence does not establish.
- Keep attention, trial, and adoption distinct. Choose hero demonstrations for their
  ability to change perception; require job relevance only when the next step needs it.
- Give social, editorial, education, and other channels distinct jobs that reinforce the
  same message instead of repeating the same copy on every surface.
- Carry a distinctive, verified proof idea across the few channels where it can do a
  different job. Use social to make the result surprising and lifecycle email to turn
  the same use case into a concrete first task; preserve the underlying request, action,
  and result across both.
- Name the customer and first champion separately when they differ.
- Treat Douglas's edited language as canonical. When he rejects an edit, restore it and
  propagate the decision through the authorized scope.
- Preserve parentheticals, fragments, jokes, and rough one-two-punch language when they
  carry the idea or make it producible.
- Distinguish an approval-level film concept from a shootable treatment. The latter needs
  timed beats, exact spoken copy, visible action, production notes, and an honest runtime.
- Give a hero film one story or comic engine. Put product proof inside the character beat
  and make the strategic mechanism happen on screen before the closing line explains it.
- Give a recurring brand series a fixed engine and variable episodes. In very short films,
  use performance, props, setting, and silence to carry character while the product stays
  visible and the spoken copy stays sparse.
- Write proof as request, action, and result. Explain causal links and retrieve missing
  facts instead of replacing them with mystery language.
- When the product collapses a laborious task, show the steps a person would have to
  complete before revealing the one agent request and finished result. Keep the steps
  true to the task and avoid inventing an internal process.
- Give adjacent social posts different jobs. If one post dramatizes a result, the next
  can teach the workflow by publishing the input, exact prompt, guardrails, review step,
  and output. A renamed retelling is still a duplicate.
- Verify and tag a named teammate's public handle when they appear in public copy. The
  attribution also creates a natural repost and reply path. Never guess the handle.
- Never use “real” as credibility filler. Name the exact fact or outcome.
- Keep strategy, content/social, and creator/community plans separate when each needs its
  own operating detail.
- Compress only after the ideas are stable. Cut repeated wording, never the idea.

## Thesis-specific examples

Do not turn these into universal requirements:

- Provoke + Promote.
- “After Automation, ______?”
- Dan leaning toward provocation and Every leaning toward promotion.
- A 14-day launch window.
- Speaker and attendee nominations.
- Thesis Statements and the conference launching together.
- TBPN-style speaker cards.
- Famous bad predictions.
- Classroom practitioners and education products.

## Every Agent–specific examples

Do not turn these into universal requirements:

- “Make your team AI-native.”
- “Show, don't tell.”
- “Every, installed.”
- The AI ambassador as the first champion.
- The company agent as the category.
- Slack as the public work surface.
- Compound Engineering and compounding as product mechanisms.
- Separate strategy, content/social, and creator/community one-pagers.
- September as Agent Month.
- Creator seeding, token credits, and affiliate mechanics.

## Corrections that should become eval traps

- Assigning a contributor to a subject their statement does not address.
- Inventing or misreading a person's expertise.
- Using the wrong date.
- Treating a pricing question as a locked decision.
- Focusing on application emails before the launch story is clear.
- Producing a long social operating manual when the user asked for a brief.
- Rewriting protected copy.
- Replacing Douglas's edited line with a smoother paraphrase in the next pass.
- Making one positioning paragraph repeat the same outcome as audience, mechanism, and
  benefit.
- Turning a specific product receipt into “real work,” “a hidden issue,” or another vague
  capability claim.
- Naming the request but omitting what the product did or what changed.
- Removing a parenthetical or rough phrase that carries the creative idea.
- Pulling pricing or measurement to the center before the positioning is settled.
- Combining the strategy, content/social plan, and creator/community plan into one
  overloaded document.
- Using “room” as shorthand for attendees, speakers, or the event.
