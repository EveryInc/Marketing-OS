# Content-System GTM Approval Deck

Use this format when leadership has 10 to 15 minutes to approve a launch content
ecosystem: the campaign logic, hero asset, recurring formats, publishing cadence, and
production mandate. The deck is the presentation layer of the governing GTM plan. It
does not replace the full strategy or become a slide-by-slide transcript of it.

This is a working approval deck, not a keynote. Use minimal section cards to pace the
review, but keep the concrete examples, operating logic, and production detail required
to make a decision. One decision per slide matters more than an arbitrary word limit.

## Required inputs

Do not draft until the governing GTM decision identifies the audience behavior, campaign
idea, primary action, protected language, proof standard, approval owner, and known
production constraints. The source packet must also supply the content-role mix or leave
it visibly open. If any missing input could change the system leadership is approving,
stop and surface it. For missing production values that do not change the system, mark
them open and continue.

## Start with the decision

Write the approval ask in one sentence before outlining slides. Name:

- The complete system leadership is approving.
- The production volume or mandate that approval unlocks.
- The few choices leadership still needs to make.
- What the team will own after approval.

List each open input atomically before counting it. “Two workflows” is two selections,
not one category. Use the same count and labels in the opening decision, burn-down, and
closing approval ask.

Do not make leadership re-approve every post, workflow, or cutdown when the decision is
to approve the system and authorize production. Separate system approval from the
smaller inputs still required, such as choosing claims, workflows, hosts, or a working
campaign name.

## Build five acts

### 1. Decision and behavior

Open with the launch promise, then make the strategy scannable:

1. **Goal:** Audience, behavior change, primary action, intended perception, and one
   consistent CTA.
2. **Approach:** The launch as one content ecosystem, including the roles of brand,
   product, proof, education, editorial, community, and creators when relevant.
3. **Jobs to be done:** The small set of questions the market must be able to answer.
4. **Governing creative rule:** A direction such as show, demonstrate, dramatize, or
   work in public that can judge every proposed asset.

The goal slide states what changes in the audience. The approach slide explains how the
system creates that change. Do not repeat the same claim under different labels.

### 2. Campaign sequence

Show the causal phases before the content inventory. Use the phases the GTM decision
requires, usually three to five. Give each phase one job.

For every phase, state:

- **Goal:** The audience condition the phase should create.
- **Actions:** The few specific things the team will publish or do.
- **Proof:** The receipt, workflow, person, or result that makes the phase credible.
- **CTA:** The next action, kept consistent unless the strategy requires a change.

Prelaunch content can tease verified behavior, tension, or proof. It cannot depend on
launch reactions, product knowledge, or audience participation that does not exist yet.

### 3. Content system

Organize content by strategic job rather than by a brainstormed list of assets:

- **Hero launch film:** Makes the campaign idea and product memorable.
- **Product-led formats:** Each proves one customer advantage through one workflow.
- **Brand-led formats:** Repeatable entertainment that dramatizes the product tension.
- **Proof:** Customer, internal, beta, creator, or partner receipts.
- **Education:** Teaches the behavior required to use the category well.
- **Editorial:** Establishes authority, explains the category, or supplies independent
  evidence and perspective.
- **One-off ideas:** Keep optional experiments on one slide so they do not compete with
  the approved recurring system.

Give the hero film its own slide. Give each recurring format a short slide or compact
card that states the premise, action, product reveal, and repeatable variation. A title
is not an idea. An unfamiliar reader should understand what happens on screen.

Separate the brand film from the proof library. The brand film makes the idea memorable;
product and proof formats make the offer credible. Every product clip should prove one
claim rather than tour several features.

## Write at production specificity

Use plain language. Every example should name:

1. The request or tension.
2. What the product or participant does.
3. The finished result or changed state.

Translate internal shorthand into audience-facing meaning. Write “Update the website
with the new speaker” rather than “speaker PR.” Write “A short clip from the launch film”
rather than “tomorrow, meet the product.” If the evidence is incomplete, use `TBD`,
`select case`, or an open question. Never invent a cast member, location, owner, customer,
workflow, or publishing fact to fill a box.

Use one governing CTA across the system unless a different action is strategically
necessary. Treat front cards, end cards, captions, cutdowns, ratios, and source-file
archiving as production deliverables. Put a cutdown on the publishing calendar only when
it has a distinct audience job, not because the file exists.

### 4. Distribution and calendar

#### Turn the system into a credible calendar

The calendar proves the strategy can operate. Make it native and editable.

- Translate the approved cadence and any explicitly supplied content-role mix into named
  content roles. If the source does not set a weekly mix, mark that allocation open.
- Preserve the required balance between product, brand, proof, education, editorial,
  and community. Do not leave one role until the end of the month.
- Cluster installments of a recurring series closely enough for the audience to learn
  the format.
- Launch supporting cuts or follow-ups near the master asset when they serve a new job.
- Use weekends only when channel behavior or the idea justifies them. A blank day is
  stronger than filler.
- Schedule questions, reactions, recaps, and audience participation only after the
  campaign has created something people can respond to.
- Give every cell literal copy: the asset or action, the claim or workflow, and any
  dependency needed to understand it.
- Mark uncertain cases and dates instead of pretending they are locked.

Color-code by strategic role, not by arbitrary visual variety. Keep the legend stable.
Use the brand type system, align the text boxes to their copy, and verify that every label
remains readable at presentation size.

#### Keep channel strategy light

The deck should explain how the same asset system changes by channel, not repeat the
calendar for every platform.

- State which channel carries the complete video conversation.
- State which channel selects education, editorial, proof, or professional context.
- Name any native formats worth testing, such as slideshows.
- Clarify that shared assets can use different copy, emphasis, or framing.

Leave response manuals, exhaustive matrices, and post-level copy in the operating plan.

### 5. Burn-down and approval

Replace a repetitive summary with the work required to authorize and ship the system.
A useful close uses four workstreams:

1. **Lock:** Strategy, claims, formats, offer, dates, and decisions.
2. **Make:** Hero asset, recurring formats, templates, captures, and production finishes.
3. **Publish:** Editorial, education, proof, social packages, permissions, and cadence.
4. **Ship:** Distribution, conversion surfaces, QA, measurement, owners, and review date.

Keep each item atomic and written as an action. Separate deferred work so it cannot be
mistaken for launch scope. End with the exact approvals or selections required from
leadership, followed by discussion.

## Review-budget test

Budget the 10 to 15 minutes around decisions, not slide count:

- **Three to four minutes:** Goal, approach, market jobs, and campaign sequence.
- **Six to eight minutes:** Hero asset and the recurring content system.
- **Three to four minutes:** Channel role, calendar, burn-down, and approval ask.

Section cards may move quickly. Put overflow detail in an appendix or the master GTM
plan. If leadership must debate the underlying audience, offer, positioning, or campaign
idea, the deck arrived before the strategy was ready.

## Final check

- [ ] The exact review decision appears near the beginning and again at the close
- [ ] Goal, approach, and jobs to be done perform different strategic roles
- [ ] Campaign phases precede the asset inventory
- [ ] The hero film, product-led formats, and brand-led formats are distinct
- [ ] Proof, education, and editorial each have an explicit job
- [ ] Every example states request, action, and result
- [ ] Every calendar cell is literal, sequenced, and justified
- [ ] The approved cadence and any supplied role mix appear throughout the calendar
- [ ] No invented owner, talent, case, date, or workflow fills a gap
- [ ] The opening and close use the same atomic list and count of open inputs
- [ ] Social roles remain shorter than the content system they distribute
- [ ] The close states what to lock, make, publish, and ship
- [ ] Fonts, colors, alignment, and editable objects match the native deck
