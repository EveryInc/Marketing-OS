# Strategy Decks

Use for Douglas's substantive brand, marketing, and always-on social strategy pitches.
These are presentation standards, not a replacement for the governing strategy. A
one-slide update or narrow repair does not require a new pitch architecture. An explicit
brief overrides the default sequence.

## Build an argument before an operating plan

Establish the business ambition, audience tension, evidence, and strategic choice before
content buckets, channel tactics, staffing, or cadence. State what the brand will become
known for, why it can own that position, and what it will decline to do. A supporting
program, such as employee publishing, cannot become the hero strategy by default.

Use this default flow:

1. Title and central ambition.
2. Contents: the argument's chapters, not a list of unrelated topics.
3. Opportunity: audience problem, cultural or category tension, evidence, consequence.
4. Strategy: distinctive point of view, governing choice, organizing idea, refusals.
5. Expression and distribution: concrete formats, examples, channel and participant roles.
6. Plan and measurement: ownership, capacity, sequence, conversion path, tests, decisions.
7. Closing summary, proposed next steps, and a final Thank you slide.

Use chapter breaks where the argument changes altitude. Keep detailed definitions,
operating matrices, and supporting research in an appendix or linked working plan. If an
appendix sits inside the delivered file, end the complete deck with the closing sequence
unless the brief specifies a different order.

For a short launch content-system approval deck, the five-act structure in
`marketing/gtm/references/content-system-approval-deck.md` remains the owner. Apply these
brand, clean-slide, and closing standards without layering on a second outline.

## Write the claim, then prove it

Use sharp, declarative headlines that make one consequential point. Support them with
specific data, a demonstrated behavior, a named example, or social proof. Show the
request, action, and result when explaining a product or workflow. An ownable idea must
generate executions; a slogan alone is insufficient.

Separate observed evidence from proposed concepts. Identify sample, period, denominator,
and attribution limits where they change the claim. Engagement rankings do not prove
subscriber growth or incremental revenue. Missing baselines and targets remain open.

Always-on strategy needs a workable operating model: owners or proposed roles, capacity,
publishing rhythm, a route to subscription, and rules for continuing, changing, or ending
a test. These support the strategic argument; they do not replace it.

## Keep slide faces clean

Do not place assistant commentary, drafting instructions, source-retrieval logs, skill
names, QA status, editorial caveats about the work process, or production notes on slide
faces. Do not hide them in smaller footers or speaker notes. Douglas permits no added
annotations unless explicitly requested. Keep research provenance and private process
records in local working files. Apply the preflight and readback checks in
`native-document-editing.md`, including for narrow edits and newly duplicated slides.

Retain qualifications that change meaning inside the claim itself: for example,
“proposed next steps,” “tracked signups,” or the dates of a measured period. Moving a
qualification out of view must not turn a proposal into an approval or an association
into a causal result. Essential instructions in a requested production-plan slide are
content, not removable annotations. Brand marks, page numbers, and intentional navigation
are not editorial notes.

## Match the approved brand before delivery

Inspect the current brand files and relevant prior decks. Use the actual fonts, logos,
color system, imagery, composition, and typographic hierarchy. Where Douglas asks for
his previous client decks as flow references, borrow the argument architecture, not
private client claims or their identities. Do not substitute a generic template and
expect a later brand pass to make it acceptable.

Give section breaks different approved background images within one coherent system.
Keep chapter typography consistent. Check each crop separately, including faces and
important symbols; preserve image proportions. A repeated background is not sufficient
variation merely because the chapter number changes.

## Close with decisions and actions

The summary restates the ambition, governing choice, and intended business consequence.
It does not introduce another strategy. Next steps identify the decisions, deliverables,
proposed owners, dependencies, and timing needed to start. Do not invent approval or
staff availability. The final slide says Thank you.

## Verify without restarting the work

Preserve native editable objects and unaffected content. For a narrow revision, reuse
approved sources and prior verification where unchanged; do not repeat the research or
redesign the deck. Batch compatible edits, read back the saved revision, and inspect the
changed rendered slides. Check the full visual sequence after a substantial rebuild.

Review against `../evals/strategy-deck-regressions.json`. Preserve the distinction between
a self-reviewed scenario test and independently observed future performance.
