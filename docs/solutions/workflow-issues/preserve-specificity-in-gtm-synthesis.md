# Preserve Specificity in GTM Synthesis

## Context

Collaborative GTM work degrades when synthesis treats every source as equally
authoritative. Product facts, executive strategy, canonical copy, proof, and execution
details get blended into one polished summary. Approved lines are paraphrased, specific
examples become generic claims, distinct strategic layers collapse, and the document
grows repetitive while saying less.

The failure is a breakdown in authority, altitude, and evidence. A product fact starts
doing the job of a strategy. A supporting UVP becomes the hero. A specific Slack receipt
becomes “real work.” Pricing or measurement takes over before the product story is
settled. The fix is to establish what each source owns, lock the strategic spine,
preserve the document owner's edits, and demand proof-level specificity. Compression
comes last.

## Guidance

### Establish authority

Assign a governing source to product truth, strategy, canonical copy, proof, structure,
execution, and approval. One source may own several roles, but none owns everything by
default. When sources conflict, name the conflict and the winning authority before
drafting.

Retrieve specifics from Slack, Notion, and Drive instead of filling gaps with plausible
language. If the request, action, result, date, owner, or artifact is missing, search for
it or mark it open.

### Lock the strategic spine

Settle two lines before calendars, creator programs, content plans, pricing explanations,
or measurement systems:

1. A one-line strategy stating how the launch will win.
2. A positioning statement naming the audience and tension, product category, outcome,
   mechanism, and reason to believe.

Make each sentence and section do one job. Keep the product promise, positioning, hero
UVP, supporting UVPs, mechanism, reason to believe, and proof distinct. Name the customer
and first champion separately when they differ.

### Preserve human-edited language

Treat the document owner's edits as canonical. Compare the versions, classify the
change, and carry the lesson forward. When an edit is rejected, restore the chosen
wording immediately and reconcile dependent language within the authorized scope.

Preserve parentheticals, fragments, provocations, and rough one-two-punch rhythms when
they carry voice or make an idea producible. Tighten the connective prose around them;
do not smooth the identity out of the document.

### Require request, action, and result

Every case study and product example should answer:

1. What did the person ask?
2. What did the product do?
3. What changed?

Explain causal links an unfamiliar reader cannot infer. Never replace a specific field,
file, error, fix, artifact, or outcome with mystery language. Do not use “real” as
credibility filler; name the exact fact instead.

### Separate plans by altitude

Keep the strategy one-pager, content/social one-pager, and creator/community one-pager
separate when each needs operating detail. The strategy locks the product story and proof
system. The linked plans translate it into creative production and distribution.

Record pricing and measurement facts without letting them displace an unresolved
positioning decision. Expand those sections only when they are the assignment.

### Compress last

Cut repetition, throat-clearing, stale context, and process detail after the strategic
argument and canonical language are stable. Preserve every unique audience insight,
mechanism, proof point, owner, cultural reference, creative detail, and unresolved
decision. If the shorter version sounds like something any company could say, reverse
the cut.

### Edit native documents locally

Fetch the latest collaborative document, edit only the authorized section, and re-fetch
after writing. Verify the intended change, protected language, surrounding content, and
native formatting. Never rebuild a live strategy document from an earlier export.

## Why This Matters

GTM documents coordinate product, editorial, design, creators, leadership, and
distribution. Generic language cannot direct those teams. A locked strategy tells the
content team which stories to seek. A defined champion tells the creator team whom to
recruit. A clear mechanism tells product what must be demonstrable. A
request-action-result standard tells design which frames to capture.

Abstraction compounds in the other direction too. Once vague language enters the master
plan, the website, film, creator brief, social posts, support materials, and launch
article repeat it. The campaign becomes consistent without becoming distinctive.

## When to Apply

- Building or revising a GTM strategy, positioning document, or launch brief
- Compressing a full strategy into an executive one-pager
- Turning product activity into case studies or campaign proof
- Working from several sources with different authority
- Revising a document after the owner sharpened the language
- Editing a live Notion page with protected language and formatting
- Splitting strategy, content/social, and creator/community workstreams

## Examples

### Keep the strategy to one choice

Before:

> Bring compounding to the agent, use it as a vessel for product experiments, and help
> companies stay at the edge of AI.

After:

> **Strategy:** Win the AI ambassador, then use them to make the company-agent category
> ours.

The surrounding document can explain the experiment pipeline. The strategy states the
choice.

### Preserve proof

Before:

> The agent can update a website.

Illustrative structure:

> **Request:** Replace the speaker's image on the website.
>
> **Action:** Find the approved asset in Figma and update the page.
>
> **Result:** Open a pull request with the change.

The second version gives a producer three things to capture and a reader one complete
causal chain. Replace each placeholder with verified source details before publication.

### Cut repetition without cutting the idea

Before:

> The agent remembers context and corrections. The more the company uses it, the more it
> remembers. Future work improves because each correction carries forward.

After:

> Correct it once. Never think about it twice.

This works because every sentence performed the same job. A distinct idea, such as a
coworker inheriting the workflow, should remain separate.

### Preserve human voice

Before:

> Create a humorous social series contrasting the agent with undesirable coworker
> behavior.

After:

> “You don't need another coworker. You need @Every.” Show employees being bad coworkers
> (microwaving fish, clipping their nails at lunch, etc.).

The parenthetical turns a generic format into a producible idea.
