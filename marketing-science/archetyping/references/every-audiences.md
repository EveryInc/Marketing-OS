## Every (master brand)

Every uses four behavioral audience segments. They are not demographic personas and do
not create four separate brands. The company promise remains consistent; the proof,
entry point, and desired movement change.

### Builders

People actively building AI products, systems, workflows, or companies. They value
technical credibility, useful artifacts, original ideas, and a creative standard worth
identifying with.

- **Who they are:** Technical founders, engineers, and hands-on product leaders already
  working at the edge.
- **Promise:** "Know what's possible before everyone else."
- **Every's role:** The Frontier Team, Every's applied AI lab, plus a community built to
  test what comes next.
- **Brand role:** Credibility. Winning serious builders keeps Every close to the frontier
  and earns the right to lead operators.
- **Commercial role:** Evals, alpha testing, and early-access programs are the long-term
  opportunity.

### Operators

People applying AI inside a role, team, or organization. They want practical workflows,
credible peers, and evidence that helps them work differently. Marketing deepens
affinity and paid conversion by making Every useful, distinctive, and culturally
valuable.

- **Who they are:** Usually director-level or senior functional leaders turning AI into
  better work inside an existing company.
- **Promise:** "Be the AI guy."
- **Every's role:** A learning system that turns ideas, software, and education into
  repeatable practice.
- **Brand role:** Badge value. "I read Every" signals that they take AI seriously and
  know how to put it to work.
- **Commercial role:** Subscription scale. Operators are the center of the
  one-subscription business.

### Seekers

People who know AI matters but have not yet built a working practice with it. They need
a clear gateway, accessible entry points, and a reason to begin now. Marketing expands
awareness and turns discovery into free subscriptions. Over time, Seekers can become
Builders or Operators.

Seekers are an audience to watch rather than a core segment. Do not build a separate
position around them or make them feel behind.

### Executives

Decision-makers responsible for organizational adaptation. They need authority,
proprietary evidence, applied expertise, and visible client proof. Marketing expands
awareness and creates Consulting demand through research, public relations, events, and
expert visibility.

- **Who they are:** CEOs, other C-suite leaders, and heads of major business units.
- **Promise:** "Make AI real inside my company."
- **Every's role:** A transformation partner that brings Every's learning system inside
  the organization.
- **Brand role:** Authority. Enterprise work proves that Every can move from individual
  practice to organizational change.
- **Commercial role:** Enterprise revenue. An engagement may come first; the
  subscription follows.

## Cross-brand audience overlaps

- Builders and Operators form Every's current core and are more likely to know the brand.
- Seekers are the largest underdeveloped awareness and free-subscriber opportunity.
- Executives overlap with Operators but enter through authority, business evidence, and
  enterprise work. An engagement may be their first relationship with Every.
- Product-level audience definitions refine this model without replacing it.
