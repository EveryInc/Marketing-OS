# @every social copy

Use this overlay when writing, revising, or evaluating posts for Every's primary X
account. It also applies to employee repost copy intended for `@every`. Load
`craft/compound-copywriting/references/social-learning.md` first for general social
craft.

This is an account-specific prior. Do not apply it wholesale to Every product accounts,
founder accounts, employee accounts, clients, or other brands.

## Evidence and scope

Douglas established this standard on Sept. 18, 2026, after reviewing the 10 most-liked
`@every` posts within five positive Jev labels, using a score threshold of 0.5: strong
hook, how-to, teaches something, product or Every promotion, and personal story.

The recurring winners stacked attributes. A strong opening worked best when it led to a
guide, course, technique, or first-hand Every story. Promotional posts traveled when the
release taught something useful or came with evidence from work Every had done.

Likes identify posts worth studying. They do not establish the cause of performance or
prove subscriptions, product use, or revenue. Match measurement to the post's assigned
job.

## Core formula

**Earn attention → teach one concrete thing from work Every has done → land in agents,
writing with AI, compound engineering, or AI-native work.**

The opening creates the pull. The payoff earns it.

## Topic priorities

Default to:

1. Agents and AI-native work, including Claude Code, compound engineering, and harnesses.
2. Practical instruction, including guides, camps, pro tips, prompts, slash commands,
   and repeatable workflows.
3. Lived Every experiments with a clear action, duration or condition, and result.
4. Product and guide releases that teach something useful, including Compound Writing,
   the Builder Pack, and Every apps.

Deprioritize pure news reactions, unsupported hot takes, and generic audience questions.
Use one when the brief supplies a sharper reason than the default, such as a timely joke,
a consequential announcement, or a distinctive cultural observation.

## Two proven shapes

### Playbook

Name the common mistake or missed move, then teach the better approach. Give the reader
enough detail to use it. A claim without the move is commentary; steps without a sharp
claim are documentation.

### Field note

Report what Every tried, what failed or worked, and what changed. Preserve the request,
action, and result. The story earns authority because Every did the work.

Do not force every post into either shape. They are strong defaults for the primary
account, not mandatory templates.

## Build the post

1. **Choose one job.** Teach, demonstrate, explain, announce, entertain, or tell a story.
2. **Choose one idea.** A post can contain several details when they all serve the same
   point.
3. **Open with the signal.** Use a claim, tension, number, confession, result, or specific
   surprise.
4. **Deliver the payoff.** Give the step, mechanism, failure, result, example, or changed
   practice promised by the opening.
5. **Return to Every's lane.** Make the connection to agents, writing with AI, compound
   engineering, or AI-native work clear without adding an abstract moral.

Use specific facts and numbers when they help the reader understand or believe the
point. Sound like an insider sharing useful work, not a marketer polishing a content
calendar. Confident promotion is welcome when the post teaches.

## When the job is qualified traffic

A traffic post must still be useful in-feed. Give the reader the conclusion or result,
then make the site visit valuable by naming material that cannot fit usefully in the
post: the test, prompt, skill, workflow, data, code, screenshots, comparison, or failure
log behind it.

For an important article or guide, use this as the default treatment to test:

1. **Native reach post:** Open with the strongest claim, result, tension, or
   demonstration. Include one concrete proof point. Use media when it helps show the
   result.
2. **Context-matched linked reply or follow-up:** State exactly what the destination
   contains and who will benefit, then link directly to it.
3. **Fresh second angle:** Return to the same destination within 48 hours with a
   different result, use case, or piece of evidence. Do not repost the same summary.

Use this traffic-copy sequence:

**Specific result or tension → one proof point → exact destination promise → link**

Replace generic calls such as “read more,” “full story,” and “check it out” with the
specific reason to click. The post and destination must keep the same promise. Deep-link
to the relevant page or section instead of sending the reader to a homepage.

This is an Every testing prior, not a platform law. External-link posts can receive less
distribution, and Every's reply-led traffic has been promising, but current post-level
tracking does not establish that putting the link in a reply causes better results.
Compare this treatment against a direct-link main post across comparable pieces.

Instrument each placement separately:

- Use one UTM for each main post, reply, and follow-up.
- Use qualified sessions from X per impression as the primary decision metric.
- Define a qualified session before the test using an observable action such as 30
  active seconds, 50% scroll, newsletter signup, or a product or subscription click.
- Diagnose performance with link clicks per impression, loaded sessions per click, free
  signups per session, and downstream paid conversion.
- Protect qualified reach and share rate as guardrails. A traffic treatment should not
  win by attracting low-intent clicks or collapsing distribution.

## Employee repost copy

The repost caption must add a useful frame instead of paraphrasing the employee or
offering praise alone.

- Find the one detail in the employee's post that changes how the reader can work.
- State the lesson, move, or implication Every can credibly add.
- Keep the employee's evidence visible; do not replace it with abstract AI commentary.
- Avoid repeating the post's full setup. Use the caption to sharpen the opening or name
  the transferable lesson.
- Keep one idea. Skip generic applause, audience questions, and corporate celebration.

## Account and partner tags

The publishing account is the speaker, not a third party.

- On posts published by `@every`, never write `@Every` in the body or CTA. Use `Every`,
  `the Every Agent`, `we`, or `our` as the sentence requires.
- Tag named employees, authors, collaborators, partners, and platforms when their work or
  product is material to the post and the handle is known. In particular, write Slack as
  `@SlackHQ` when Slack is part of the workflow, partnership, or story.
- Keep established partner handles such as `@AnthropicAI`, `@NotionHQ`, `@figma`, and
  `@github` when those companies or products are named in the copy.
- Do not invent a handle. If the correct handle is not already established and checking
  it would delay a bounded task, use the plain name and flag the missing tag.

Run the handle pass before delivery: identify the publishing account, remove every
self-tag, then check each named external person and company for the appropriate tag.

## Review gates

Before publishing, answer:

1. **Opening:** Does line one contain a claim, tension, number, confession, result, or
   specific surprise?
2. **Payoff:** Does the post teach or show one concrete thing?
3. **Evidence:** Does the claim come from a guide, build, test, observed workflow, or
   named result?
4. **Every lane:** Does the post strengthen Every's authority in agents, writing with AI,
   compound engineering, or AI-native work?
5. **Voice:** Does it sound informed, generous, confident, and slightly rough rather
   than polished into generic marketing language?
6. **Focus:** Can the reader repeat the post's one idea?
7. **Tags:** Is `@every` treated as the speaker, with no `@Every` self-tag, and are named
   partners and collaborators tagged when their handles are known?

Revise when the source is strong and the caption is vague. Hold the post when neither
the source nor the caption offers a useful idea. Approve an exception when humor,
cultural relevance, or major news is the post's deliberate job and the execution clears
the general social review.

## Measurement

### Strategy hierarchy

Every's social ambition is to make Every famous for helping people learn how to use AI
as fast as it changes, specifically for work. Audience growth and engagement should
support more free subscriptions and, over time, more paid subscriptions. This is a path
to test and measure, not a claim that each stage automatically causes the next.

Employee visibility is a supporting distribution program, not the whole strategy. Give
people distinctive subjects and evidence from their work; do not make them interchangeable
amplifiers of brand copy. The Frontier Team is a core content source and bucket. Leave
room for behind-the-scenes brand and design work, thought leadership, and criticism when
they strengthen the same promise. Education-led does not mean utility-only.

For subscription-growth programs, use tracked free subscriptions as the primary outcome,
with reach, follower growth, shares, and qualified visits as diagnostics. Follow paid
conversion and retention by mature cohort. Pair attribution with brand recognition,
authority, and qualitative audience signals; last-click results do not measure all of
marketing's contribution. A bounded traffic-copy test can retain its session-based
decision metric above without replacing the program's subscription objective.

Define virality and shareability separately. Virality describes observed spread relative
to a stated account baseline and time window. Shareability describes the reader's reason
to pass something on: practical use, a useful argument, recognition, surprise, or social
identity. Neither is established by a universal likes threshold. Jev rankings identify
creative patterns and hypotheses; note the sample, period, account size, and selection
bias before drawing conclusions.

### Post-level measures

Match the metric to the post's job:

- Attention or cultural relevance: qualified reach, shares, replies, and follower growth.
- Teaching: saves, shares, link visits, and use of the guide, prompt, or workflow.
- Product or membership: verified visits, subscriptions, installs, activations, or other
  product actions.
- Brand authority: citations, invitations, press pickup, and qualitative response from
  the intended audience.

Use likes as a resonance signal. Never treat them as the only measure of success.
