# Wildcard

One idea, not a list. A wildcard is the move a challenger would make and an incumbent
would not: large upside, a fair chance of not working, and a test cheap enough that
failing costs little. It sits beside the plan. It never replaces the plan or enters its
forecast.

## When to invoke

- When a plan is sound but safe, and the goal is larger than the plan's own estimates
- When someone asks for a wildcard, a swing, or what a challenger brand would do
- After the research and the core plan exist. A wildcard built before the evidence is a
  guess.

## Inputs

- The plan and its numbers: the goal, what the sized sources deliver, the budget, the dates
- The research already gathered, first-party data above all
- Constraints: what the brand will not do, and what cannot be built in time

If the plan has no stated goal or no sized estimate, say so and size the gap first.

## Method

1. **Start from the gap.** State the goal, what the plan's sized sources add up to, and
   the difference. The wildcard aims at the difference.
2. **Find the asset the plan underuses.** Look in the first-party data for something true
   that a competitor could not say or do: a concentration, a behavior, an audience nobody
   has addressed. The strongest wildcards come from a finding the core plan reported and
   then did nothing with.
3. **Turn the asset into a mechanism.** A wildcard is a mechanism: how one buyer brings
   the next, how a price moves, how a public number creates pressure. A slogan or a stunt
   with no mechanism is not a wildcard.
4. **Check precedent.** Search for at least five dated cases of the same mechanism with
   reported outcomes, from any category. At least one must be a failure. Prefer the last
   two years and say so when the cases are older. Record what made each work or fail. A
   case from another category is an analogy and is labeled as one. If fewer than five
   exist, state the count and lower the confidence.
5. **Size it.** Give the upside as a range, with the assumption behind each end, labeled
   as an estimate. Name the base it is a share of and what is unknown about that base.
6. **Name the ways it fails.** List the specific failure modes and what would show each
   one early: it does not catch on; it catches on and costs margin; it breaks a promise
   to existing customers; it embarrasses the brand; it creates legal or privacy exposure.
7. **Design the smallest test.** One audience, one or two weeks, a stated cost, a number
   that means continue and a number that means stop. Prefer a test that can be run by
   hand before anything is built.
8. **State what it needs.** Owner, approvals, anything to build, and the last date it can
   start.

## The bar

- Exactly one idea. Alternatives considered get one line each, with why they lost.
- It is something the core plan would not include on its own.
- If it works, it changes the plan's outcome. A gain smaller than the plan's largest
  sized source is a tactic, not a wildcard.
- Failing is survivable: capped cost, no lasting harm to existing customers or the brand.
- Evidence follows `marketing-science/research/references/evidence-standard.md`.
- The wildcard stays outside the plan's numbers until a test result exists.

## Output

One page, in this order:

1. The idea in one sentence
2. Why now: the finding it is built on, with its source and date
3. How it works: the mechanism in three to five steps
4. Upside: the range, the assumptions, labeled as an estimate
5. Precedent: each case with its date and outcome, the failure included
6. Risks and the early sign of each
7. The test: audience, cost, dates, the continue number and the stop number
8. What it needs and the decision required

## Quick checklist

- [ ] One idea, built on a finding in the plan's own research
- [ ] A mechanism, not a slogan
- [ ] Five dated precedents with one failure, or the shortfall stated
- [ ] Upside given as a labeled estimate with its assumptions
- [ ] Failure modes and their early signs named
- [ ] Smallest test defined, with continue and stop numbers
- [ ] Kept outside the plan's forecast
