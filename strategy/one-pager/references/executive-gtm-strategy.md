# Executive GTM Strategy

Turn deep product and brand strategy into a document leadership can review in 10 to 15
minutes and use to make decisions.

Before drafting, run the project-authority interview in
`foundation/marketing-os/references/collaboration.md`. Product truth, strategic authority,
canonical copy, structure, formatting, evidence, approval, and publication may each have
different owners.

## Five-part structure

1. **Executive summary:** State the decision, market tension, why now, why this product,
   and why the company can win.
2. **Audience:** Name the initial cohorts, their shared problem, and the decision rule for
   choosing the launch lead.
3. **First successful use:** Show the first useful result, intended reaction, feedback
   loop, and repeat behavior that proves the mechanism.
4. **Messaging architecture:** Lock the category shift, tension, promise, positioning,
   campaign line, shorthand, value proposition, mechanism, reason to believe, and proof.
5. **Launch plan:** Separate locked decisions, beta experiments, proof required for
   public launch, and open questions.

Lead with decisions and intended behavior. Use evidence only when it changes the audience,
message, channel, experiment, investment, or launch gate. Cut feature inventories,
repeated summaries, generic theory, and evidence that merely decorates an argument.

## First successful use

Prefer concrete language such as "first successful use," "first run," or "first
activation path" over internal growth jargon unless the team already uses it.

- The first run produces a useful artifact and creates the intended emotional response,
  often an instant aha moment.
- The customer accepts, rejects, or corrects the work.
- The system retains approved learning.
- The second run improves and proves the differentiated mechanism.
- Continued runs turn repeated expert judgment into reusable company capability.

Preserve the distinction: the first run creates desire and trust; the second proves
compounding.

## Messaging discipline

Separate the hierarchy:

- **Lead:** Customer outcome and differentiated mechanism.
- **Reason to believe:** Provenance, production use, and credible operating experience.
- **Proof:** Workflows, runs, corrections, accepted learnings, artifacts, and measurable
  subsequent improvement.
- **Support:** Libraries, bundles, archives, education, integrations, memory, and other
  capabilities that substantiate the proposition.
- **Parity:** Expected category features that should not become the lead message.

Do not force one product noun to explain role, mechanism, and company-wide outcome. Use a
familiar role for comprehension, then explain the mechanism and consequence separately.

## Creator seeding

Treat seeded partners as operator-creators selected for expertise and audience fit.
Distribution is a benefit, not the sole qualification.

Each partnership should aim to produce:

- One real, load-bearing workflow.
- A credible first-run artifact and second-run improvement.
- A proven workflow suitable for a library when appropriate.
- A publishable demonstration in the creator's voice.
- Distribution to a qualified audience.

Measure qualified workspaces and second-run completion from each partnership. Treat reach
and engagement as diagnostics.

## Executive test

The document is ready when leadership can identify:

- The audience and why it leads.
- The first behavior the launch must create.
- The promise, mechanism, reason to believe, and proof.
- What is locked, what is being tested, and what remains open.
- The evidence required to approve public launch.
- The metric and decision rule that will choose the next move.
