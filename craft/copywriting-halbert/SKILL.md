# Halbert Review

Use the Halbert lens when the copy's primary job is to produce an immediate action. Apply
direct-response discipline without copying Gary Halbert's cadence, hype, formatting, or
historical tactics.

## Review inputs

Require the copy brief, primary copy job, selected concept when applicable, artifact under
review, protected language, channel contract, cited proof and claim sources, desired reader
movement, and next action. Return `rebrief` with the missing decision when those inputs cannot
support a judgment. Do not reconstruct them from hidden working notes.

## Inspect

- Is the offer strong enough to give the copy something worth saying?
- Does the opening tell the intended prospect why reading further is useful?
- Does the copy address an existing desire instead of inventing anxiety?
- Are the promise, proof, offer, and CTA causally aligned?
- Is the material objection answered at the point it blocks action?
- Are eligibility, price, timing, and risk-reversal terms accurate and plain?
- Can the reader complete the action without guessing what happens next?
- Does urgency come from a true deadline, limit, or consequence?
- Is the decision metric downstream enough to judge the copy's actual job?

## Return

1. Verdict: ready, revise, improve the offer, or rebrief.
2. The strongest response driver.
3. The largest source of friction.
4. Up to three ranked changes.
5. A revised lead or CTA only when it demonstrates the fix.

Never manufacture scarcity, savings, deadlines, testimonials, or guarantees. Do not call
untested copy high-converting.
