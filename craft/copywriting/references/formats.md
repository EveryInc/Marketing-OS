# Copywriting Formats

Choose a form that serves the reader's decision. Brand style, the brief, and protected
human language govern execution. See `creative-canon.md` for evidence and precedents.

## Headlines, email subjects, and social hooks

1. Decide what earns attention: desired change, recognizable tension, consequential news,
   concrete proof, useful specificity, or an observation with personality. Pick the
   strongest supported approach; these are options, not slots to fill.
2. Write the promise plainly, then develop meaningfully different approaches. Changing
   adjectives is not creative exploration. When selection is open, present a small set
   and recommend one. Once the human selects a line, preserve it.
3. Make a promise the body can pay off. Curiosity may withhold the resolution; it must
   give enough context to establish relevance. A mysterious teaser with no reader value
   is not a hook. Never use fake reply subjects or misleading personalized familiarity.
4. Keep useful information early enough to survive truncation. Read subject and preview
   together as an inbox unit. Preview text adds proof, mechanism, or offer instead of
   repeating the subject. Check the actual display; a character recommendation is not a
   law of persuasion.
5. Judge the lead on relevance, specificity, brand character, credibility, and the desire
   to read the next line. A grammatically correct category label may fail all five.
6. Direct tension at the problem. Do not insult the audience to sound provocative or
   trade a defensible ambition for manufactured fear. Use the brand's chosen edge.
7. A headline need not explain the whole product. The first body lines should quickly
   establish the product and its role in delivering the promise.

## Short body copy

Build the shortest complete argument: a reason to care, enough explanation to understand,
specific proof, and a next action. Reorder those elements when the creative idea benefits.
Use bullets when comparison or scanning helps. Each bullet should advance the proposition;
do not turn a short email into a checklist of unrelated features.

Delete the paragraph that congratulates the example on proving the point. Keep a sentence
that explains an otherwise missing cause, consequence, or practical next step. Compression
works when the reader can still say what happened and why they would want it.

## Long body copy

Use length when the decision needs demonstration, narrative, risk resolution, technical
explanation, or several substantive objections. Every section needs a distinct job. A
story can sustain attention, but defer the product or ask only for an earned dramatic
reason. Historical long-copy success does not imply that every launch needs an essay.

## Taglines and slogans

A durable brand line should direct future executions and be ownable in context. A campaign
line can be narrower and temporary. Test either against the brand's behavior and category;
do not imitate another brand's sentence pattern or transplant its cultural standing.
Judge memorability and strategic force separately from literal feature specification.

## Manifestos

Use when the job is to articulate a belief and a consequential commitment. State what the
brand believes, what it chooses because of that belief, and what it actually does. Cadence
without an argument is decoration. Do not add a manifesto to a transactional flow.

## Product copy

For product examples, keep the causal chain visible: request, action, artifact or result.
The headline may compress that chain, but it may not promise a completed job that the
example does not show. Use interface verbs people recognize and distinguish drafts,
reviewed work, executed actions, and measured outcomes. Website sequencing and proof
placement belong to the routed Compound Copywriting website reference.

## Choosing the right format

- One new capability for a familiar audience: a compact announcement with relevant proof.
- A stalled next step: one objection or demonstration and one appropriate action.
- A new category or high-consideration decision: enough argument and evidence to understand.
- A brand belief: a distinctive idea expressed across words, images, behavior, and channel.

These are starting points. A human-approved treatment, channel contract, or better creative
idea can require a different form. Judge the resulting artifact, not conformity.
