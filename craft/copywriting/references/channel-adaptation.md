# Channel Adaptation

Use with the relevant audience/platform skill. Preserve proposition, facts, and brand
character; adapt the unit of attention and next action. This is an editorial synthesis
and set of testing hypotheses. Sources and evidence classes appear in `creative-canon.md`.
Verify changing platform limits before implementation.

## Email

### Write for the reader's current stage

Separate launch, member adoption, nurture, abandonment, renewal, and transactional jobs.
An existing customer needs different reassurance from a prospect. Use the current flow's
entry, trigger, suppression, and exit definitions. Previous campaign timing is precedent,
not an automatic schedule for a new product.

### Make the inbox promise and the body work together

- Apply `formats.md` to subject and preview. A useful tension or benefit may deserve the
  subject more than a product name; a meaningful launch can also earn a direct subject.
- Pay off that promise early. Establish the product and how it helps before an extended
  origin story or setup tutorial. A launch should create desire to try it; detailed
  onboarding belongs where it helps someone start.
- Build a scannable argument. Short paragraphs and benefit-led bullets can carry a few
  specific examples. A proof story can also be the whole email. Choose the structure for
  the job instead of forcing all messages into the same template.
- Give social proof a reason to matter: relevant person, credible relationship, concrete
  use, and result. Label internal use honestly. Pair a broad benefit with the observation
  or demonstration that earns it; omit a testimonial slot if evidence is unavailable.
- Resolve the biggest obstacle to the click. State the offer, eligibility, and material
  conditions. Earn urgency from an actual deadline or event. Never invent a cutoff,
  limited supply, or guarantee to fill a conversion formula.
- Use one primary action for a single-purpose marketing email. The label should name
  what happens next and match the destination. Repeating that action in a long message
  can be appropriate; competing asks require a reason. Editorial digests and
  transactional messages can have different link needs.
- Keep the copy readable on mobile, including when images are blocked. A graphic can
  prove a result, but essential context, terms, and action must remain understandable.

### Sequence by behavior

A follow-up should supply new proof, answer an objection, or help the next unfinished
step. Do not resend the same pitch in synonyms to someone who has already converted.
Coordinate overlapping flows and preserve an exit. Use actual event history when
available. Open/non-open segmentation is noisy under privacy protection; prefer meaningful
action state where supported. Never infer that a non-open means indifference.

## Social

### Write a post, not a cropped email

- Choose the job: reach and brand memory, useful discussion, proof, qualified traffic,
  or conversion. An organic brand post need not ask for a purchase. A response ad needs
  a clear next action and a destination that delivers the promised offer.
- Give the opening a reason to stop: a recognizable situation, sharp claim, surprising
  artifact, useful answer, or specific story. Deliver value inside the post or creative
  rather than postponing it indefinitely behind a link.
- Build around one thought or demonstration. Add enough context for someone encountering
  the brand for the first time. Leave out internal process narration and assumed lore.
- Decide what copy, image, video, and caption each contribute. A product demo should show
  the request, relevant action, and result. Give the visual a job beyond decoration and
  keep the brand recognizable when the asset is reposted.
- Give people a reason to share: usefulness, recognition, amusement, a defensible point
  of view, or something they can show a colleague. Engagement bait is not a substitute.
  Research about sharing does not establish purchase lift or justify outrage for reach.
- Keep named examples and usage numbers sourced. Creator or customer language needs its
  actual context; never recast gifted access or an employee demo as independent acclaim.

### Platform and format decisions

| Surface | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn feed | Put the professional situation or claim before truncation; earn expansion with evidence or a useful argument. Use natural paragraphs. Avoid fabricated career stories, one-sentence staircase formatting, and a stock engagement question. |
| X or similar short feed | Make a single post intelligible by itself. Use a thread only when progression needs it; give each part a distinct contribution. Check the requested account and format limits rather than assuming all posts have the same cap. |
| Image or carousel | Let the first frame establish a promise and subsequent frames deliver it. Each frame should change understanding. Keep captions additive and important information legible. |
| Short video/Reels/TikTok | Establish the situation or product value in the opening seconds; show a recognizable action and payoff. Write spoken copy, on-screen text, and visual action together. Account for sound-off comprehension, UI overlays, and reading time. Paid-platform advice is a starting hypothesis for organic work. |

Do not prescribe a universal character sweet spot, hashtag count, link-in-comments trick,
posting hour, or manufactured comment prompt. Use current platform documentation for
mechanics and the account's own evidence for editorial and distribution choices.

## Conversion testing

1. Define the desired event, eligible audience, baseline, and measurement window before
   sending or publishing. Identify what change would be meaningful enough to act on.
   If those data are absent, call the first run a baseline.
2. Test a substantive creative hypothesis: different reader motivation, proof, or offer
   presentation. For a diagnostic comparison, vary one factor while holding audience,
   offer, timing, and destination comparable. A test of full creative packages is valid,
   but it cannot isolate which sentence caused the result.
3. Use randomized assignment and an adequate sample when possible. Predefine duration,
   minimum detectable effect or decision threshold, and stopping rule. Do not crown a
   winner after peeking at a handful of events. Use the measurement skill for design.
4. Choose the metric for the job. For response email, use a downstream event per delivered
   message or randomized eligible recipient, stating the denominator. Examine delivery
   differences between arms. Use qualified clicks and landing conversion as diagnostics;
   privacy opens and automated clicks can distort both. A content change cannot be judged
   by an event that occurs before someone encounters it.
5. For paid response social, inspect qualified conversion and acquisition economics along
   with delivery and frequency. For organic work, compare like audiences and formats;
   distribution differences limit causal claims. Pair immediate action with appropriate
   brand, demand, and qualitative signals. Keep attributed and incremental outcomes separate.
6. Monitor unsubscribes, complaints, lead quality, and negative responses alongside the
   primary metric. Better click-through with worse qualified conversion is not a win
   when qualified conversion is the goal.
7. Record observation, uncertainty, and scale/revise/continue decision. Do not label
   untested copy high-converting or import a published case's uplift as a forecast.

## Website and press

Website copy supports scanning, navigation, objections, and the conversion job of each
section; use the relevant web skill and Compound Copywriting website reference. Press
copy earns a journalist's interest through actual news and evidence; use `craft/press-comms`.
Neither should inherit an email's greeting, length, or sequence by default.

## Cross-channel consistency

Keep the core proposition, identity, approved language, product facts, offer, and evidence
consistent. Change the opening, detail, format, and action for the audience and surface.
Human-selected copy remains canonical; adaptation is not permission to paraphrase a
protected line. Carry editorial lessons locally until the learning gate supports promotion.
