# Creative Canon and Evidence

Research checked September 10, 2026. Read when choosing a creative approach, repairing
generic copy, or testing a rule's provenance. The operational guidance in `principles.md`,
`formats.md`, and `channel-adaptation.md` is our synthesis. The observations below do not
establish universal conversion effects or a definitive ranking of the world's best ads.

## Read Ogilvy and Bernbach together

**Ogilvy: make the product worth wanting.** His agency's published selection of his
writing argues for a large idea, one promise, product research, specific information,
plain speech, and identifiable branding. Use the product's most persuasive truth to earn
attention. His historical research assertions are not current platform benchmarks.
Source: [Quotations of David Ogilvy](https://www.ogilvy.com/sites/g/files/dhpsjz106/files/pdfdocuments/O23_OGILVY75_Quotations%20of%20David%20Ogilvy_WEB.pdf), especially Ideas, Consumers, Research, Writing, and Television Commercials; a primary-text collection published by Ogilvy in 2023.

**Bernbach: expression is part of persuasion.** His May 15, 1947 letter warns that
technical correctness and repeating past formulas can produce mediocre ideas. The
creative choice must give the reader a reason to notice and believe. Do not use this as
an excuse to ignore evidence. Source: [reproduction of Bernbach's letter](https://www.ideatovalue.com/crea/nickskillicorn/2016/04/legendary-1947-letter-lack-creativity-advertising-even-true-2016/), including a scan of the letter. This is a primary text hosted by a secondary publisher, not a verified full-book reading.

**Attention must lead somewhere.** Bernbach's argument about interesting expression and
belief, and the original Volkswagen ad reproduced alongside it, are available in Wiley's
[publisher excerpt of Hey, Whipple, Squeeze This](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119164005.excerpt.pdf), printed pages 5–8. The excerpt combines primary ad reproduction and later practitioner commentary; it is not experimental evidence.

## Eight executions to study

The mechanism and application columns are our editorial analysis. Study why the idea fits
the product, audience, and culture. Do not copy the wording, declare the same sales effect,
or mistake an award entry for a controlled experiment.

| Execution and source | Mechanism | Application and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Hathaway's eyepatch, discussed by [Ogilvy](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/ai-end-road-ad-business) | An unexpected visual detail creates a character and story around a familiar product. | Give the brand a specific world or human presence. An arbitrary oddity pasted onto a weak proposition is decoration. The agency's historical sales account is not a forecast. |
| Volkswagen, “Think Small,” recalled by [Volkswagen](https://www.vw.com/en/newsroom/future-of-mobility/drive-bigger-hello-light.html) | Smallness becomes a confident alternative to conspicuous consumption. | Look for the category assumption the product can credibly challenge. Do not use contrarian grammar without a consequential choice. |
| Volkswagen, “Lemon,” [original ad reproduced by Wiley](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119164005.excerpt.pdf) | A rejected car's blemished trim makes quality control tangible; apparent bad news earns the quality argument. | Use a specific concession when it establishes the benefit. Do not invent a defect, conceal a material limitation, or substitute self-deprecation for proof. |
| The Economist, “I never read The Economist,” [ad reproduced by SAGE](https://us.sagepub.com/sites/default/files/upm-binaries/32952_02_Hackley_2e_CH_02.pdf), printed pages 49–50 | The attribution to an older management trainee lets the reader infer the career implication. | Let a relevant detail complete the thought. Explain the mechanism only when an unfamiliar reader actually needs it; do not borrow the campaign's snobbery indiscriminately. |
| Nike, “Dream Crazy,” [Wieden+Kennedy's campaign account](https://www.wk.com/work/nike-dream-crazy/) | Athlete stories give an ambitious belief human stakes and a recognizable brand voice. | Make aspiration visible through people and behavior. Generic motivational language cannot inherit Nike's history or credibility. |
| Patagonia, “Don't Buy This Jacket,” [Patagonia's account](https://www.patagonia.com.au/blogs/stories/dont-buy-this-jacket) | A Black Friday interruption expresses a business belief and asks for a specific consumption choice. | A provocation needs a sincere product or company commitment. Do not treat reversal or moral positioning as a conversion trick. |
| Apple, “Shot on iPhone,” [Apple's nighttime campaign account](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/01/behind-apples-new-campaign-one-night-on-iphone-7/) | The produced photograph is evidence of the capability and a desirable object in its own right. | Put an actual output where an adjective would otherwise go. Preserve the circumstances of a curated or professional demonstration. |
| Spotify, “Thanks, 2016. It's been weird,” [campaign submission to The One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/adcawards/-award/27090/thanks-2016-its-been-weird/) | Specific listening behavior becomes locally relevant humor with an unmistakable product connection. | Find a human observation in permitted data; let the detail carry the wit. Do not invent statistics or disclose identifiable private behavior. |

## Email evidence and its limits

- **Subject relevance and display.** [Mailchimp's subject guidance](https://mailchimp.com/help/best-practices-for-email-subject-lines/) favors useful, descriptive language and mobile brevity, based on its email corpus and open-rate analysis. Treat length and punctuation advice as tests, not universal creative laws. It does not establish conversion causality.
- **Experiment design.** [Mailchimp's A/B documentation](https://mailchimp.com/help/create-ab-tests/) distinguishes testing subject, sender, content, and timing; body changes should not be assessed on opens. Our downstream-event and stopping-rule requirements extend this into a decision discipline rather than copying the platform's default winner selector.
- **Measurement contamination.** [Mailchimp's bot-activity documentation](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-bot-activity/) explains that privacy systems, security scanners, and previews can inflate opens and clicks. Use filtering and conversion events where available; changing tracking settings can also break comparability over time.
- **A causal example, with boundaries.** [Sahni, Wheeler, and Chintagunta, Marketing Science, 2018](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/personalization-email-marketing-role-non-informative-advertising) reports randomized field experiments with three companies, including downstream lead and unsubscribe effects. This supports testing relevant personalization; it does not establish that adding a first name always works, or supersede privacy, voice, and context.

## Social evidence and its limits

- **Channel practice.** [LinkedIn's ad-copy guidance](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/marketing-terms/ad-copy) connects audience knowledge, funnel stage, benefit, proof, and action. It is vendor guidance for paid ads, not evidence for a universal organic-post length or link-placement trick.
- **Video structure.** [TikTok's Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) emphasizes native presentation, an early hook, brand integration, and a clear close. Its studies concern defined paid-video contexts and often recall or viewing, not purchases. Do not port the reported lifts to another platform or confuse recall with conversion.
- **Sharing motivation.** [Berger and Milkman, What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1528077) examines emotion and sharing using news articles and experiments. [Berger's Wharton interview](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/contagious-jonah-berger-on-why-things-catch-on/) discusses usefulness and social transmission. These inform creative hypotheses about why someone shares; they do not prove a social-sales formula.

## Evidence labels to preserve

Use **practitioner principle**, **campaign observation**, **platform recommendation**,
**observational association**, or **randomized experiment** when describing support.
Record the population, channel, outcome, and important limitation for a quantitative
claim. Creative precedent can improve judgment without proving incremental revenue.
Refresh platform guidance when implementation depends on it; do not rerun historical
research for every draft. Never label a new email or post high-converting before testing.
