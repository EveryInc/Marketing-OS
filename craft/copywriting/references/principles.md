# Copywriting Principles

The copywriting principles we hold at Every — synthesized from the creative canon in
Marketing OS. These standards govern the writing. Channel skills still govern the shape.

---

## Clarity

- Write the proposition in plain language before styling it.
- Use words a person would use in conversation. Prefer common words to jargon and direct
  syntax to corporate abstraction.
- Short words, sentences, and paragraphs are useful defaults, not quotas. Preserve the
  detail or rhythm the idea needs. There is no universal headline length.
- Make the intended reader response explicit before drafting. If the writer cannot name
  what the reader should think, feel, or do next, the copy is not ready.

## Specificity

- Learn the product and research the subject before reaching for a line.
- Build claims from observed detail, verified facts, and the product's actual mechanism.
  A precise human observation is stronger than a generic benefit statement.
- Check quotations and operational claims at the source. Never let a memorable line outrun
  its evidence.

## Voice fidelity

- Natural does not mean neutral. Write as this brand speaks when it is being direct,
  intelligent, and fully itself.
- Preserve selected human language. Do not replace a distinctive line with a smoother
  model-written paraphrase.
- Grammar, punctuation, and format rules may be broken deliberately when the break makes
  the idea clearer, more alive, or more memorable. A rule break without a reason is a tic.

## Rhythm and cadence

- Read important copy aloud. The ear catches stiffness, false emphasis, and sentences the
  eye forgives.
- Repetition, contrast, threes, familiar-phrase twists, and unusual headline lengths are
  available techniques, not templates. Use the device that fits the thought; do not bend
  the thought to fit the device.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. Compression creates force only when the meaning remains
  intact.

## Persuasion structure

- Give each copy unit one dominant communication job. Supporting facts may deepen or prove
  that idea; they may not compete with it.
- Lead with the most consequential reason to care for this reader in this context.
- Drama, wit, tension, and an unexpected point of view can earn attention. Ground them in
  product truth or a recognizable human observation so the device advances the sale.
- Make the next action unmistakable. When the desired outcome requires immediate,
  two-way coordination, use a direct conversation instead of expecting prose to do the
  whole job.

## Economy

- Cut any word, sentence, proof point, or section that does not clarify the idea, make it
  more memorable, support belief, or move the reader toward the intended action.
- Say one thing well. Resist the late request to "also mention" a second message unless it
  serves the first.
- Length follows the job and channel. Do not turn Ogilvy's two-page limit for internal
  memos into a universal rule for ads, essays, landing pages, or sales letters.
- For consequential work, build in distance before approval, then ask a capable colleague
  to improve it. A fresh read is part of the writing process, not cleanup after it.

## The reader's intelligence

- Make the writing simple to understand, never simplistic in thought.
- Do not make the reader decode jargon, inflated diction, or a clever device to discover
  the proposition.
- Respect attention by arriving at the point. Respect intelligence by giving the claim
  enough evidence, texture, or tension to be worth reading.
