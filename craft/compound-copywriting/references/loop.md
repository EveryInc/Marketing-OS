# Compound Copywriting Loop

Use this loop across related copy deliverables. Produce the work as normal; capture the
learning from evidence already created during drafting and review.

## 1. Frame

Build a compact copy contract:

- **Job:** What must the reader think, feel, or do?
- **Audience:** Who is making that decision, in this context?
- **Authority:** Which sources own product truth, strategy, evidence, voice, structure,
  canonical copy, approval, and publication?
- **Protected language:** Which words must survive exactly?
- **Scope:** What may change, and what remains untouched?
- **Transfer target:** Which fresh, comparable artifact will show whether the learning
  travels?

Record the start time and whether a comparable baseline exists. Do not invent a baseline.

## 2. Write

Use `craft/copywriting` and the relevant channel skill.

1. Write the plain, verified proposition before styling it.
2. Separate message inventory from finished copy.
3. Generate enough material to support a real kill rate.
4. Remove known failures before presenting options.
5. Label unresolved facts and hypotheses instead of laundering them into confident copy.
6. Preserve every supplied protected line.

Present a deliberate set, not raw volume. State which options are strongest and why.

## 3. Judge

When the human edits, rejects, selects, or restores language:

1. Preserve references to the AI draft and human-edited version.
2. Treat the human version as canonical for this project.
3. Load `craft/editing/references/compound-from-edits.md`.
4. Classify only meaningful changes:
   - Strategy or audience correction.
   - Claim, mechanism, or evidence correction.
   - Stronger point of view.
   - Protected-language restoration.
   - Specificity or cultural texture.
   - Structure, hierarchy, or channel fit.
   - Voice, rhythm, grammar, or economy.
   - One-off taste decision.
5. Explain the operation behind the change. Do not merely imitate the new length,
   vocabulary, or format.

Typos, punctuation cleanup, and formatting changes do not become material learning unless
they expose a repeated system failure.

## 4. Learn

Write one bounded carry-forward instruction for the current project. It must name:

- The future decision it changes.
- The scope where it applies.
- The condition that triggers it.
- The boundary where it stops applying.

Apply it to remaining related assets immediately. This is project-local learning, so it
does not need the durable promotion gate.

Examples:

- “For this launch’s operator-facing copy, lead with the cost of coordination before the
  product mechanism. Do not apply this ordering to the executive brief.”
- “Preserve the selected campaign line verbatim in every hero and subject-line set; adapt
  the supporting explanation by channel.”

## 5. Test

Use a fresh artifact that did not appear in the original edit. Hold the governing strategy,
audience, and brand constant while changing the copy surface.

Pass only when:

- The protected language survives.
- The corrected failure does not recur.
- The new artifact does its own channel job rather than copying the edited artifact’s form.
- No new hard failure appears.
- Human review confirms that the instruction improved or correctly constrained the work.

A rewritten version of the original artifact is not a transfer test. If no fresh artifact
exists yet, record the test as pending and make no learning claim.

## 6. Compound

Classify the evidence through the Marketing OS learning loop:

- **Durable rule:** Promote only after the gate clears.
- **Good example:** Sanitize and place in the narrowest relevant evaluation when useful.
- **Regression test:** Add the smallest case that would catch the failure next time.
- **Temporary fact:** Keep project-only.
- **One-off taste decision:** Keep project-only.

Promote the operation, not the project’s wording. Delete intake records after promotion,
rejection, or placement in a narrower evaluation.
