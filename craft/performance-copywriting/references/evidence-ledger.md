# Evidence Ledger

What the research supports about response copy, graded. Grades:

- **Strong:** large field experiments or replicated studies
- **Moderate:** controlled studies in limited contexts
- **Weak:** single tests without reported sample sizes or significance
- **Practitioner:** expert doctrine without published data

Re-check sources before quoting a number. Add findings only with a source and a grade.

| Wording features predict the better of two headlines only slightly above chance (54% vs. 50%) across 24,333 confirmatory pairs | Strong | [Registered report on Upworthy tests](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10038272/) | News headlines, 2013–15 | Do not expect wording tweaks to move results; invest in offer, angle, and clarity |
| Across 141,000 headline tests on 293 news sites, no single style feature predicted success much, and rerun winners flipped about a quarter of the time | Strong | [Nieman Lab summary](https://www.niemanlab.org/2021/10/how-a-b-testing-can-and-cant-improve-your-headline-writing/) | News context | Small tests are noisy; never generalize one test into a rule |
| First-person singular and third-person words helped headlines; first-person plural ("we") hurt | Moderate | [Registered report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10038272/) | Small effects, news style | Prefer the reader's or a person's frame over the brand's "we" |
| Negative-emotion words modestly raised clicks; positive-emotion words did not | Moderate | [Registered report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10038272/) | News; may reward negativity | Naming a real problem can help; never invent anxiety (Halbert) |
| Self-referencing question headlines beat statements in two field studies but lost in one product category | Moderate | [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/are-you-more-likely-click-headlines-are-phrased-question) | Narrow contexts; questions differed in other ways | Questions to the reader are a valid angle, not a default |

| Clarity of the offer in the first seconds outweighs persuasive technique | Practitioner | [MarketingExperiments](https://marketingexperiments.com/?p=2984) | Underlying data not published | Check that a stranger understands the offer before polishing |

| Valid creative tests need roughly 50 conversions and $300–500 per variant | Practitioner | [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/facebook-ad-creative-testing-best-practices) | Meta-specific vendor guidance | Size tests before writing variants (`test-reality.md`) |
| Paid newsletter subscribers vary widely in quality; compare engagement by source | Practitioner | [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/what-i-wish-id-known-before-spending-5000-promoting-newsletters-through-meta/) | One operator's experience | Judge copy by downstream engagement, not signups alone |

## Folklore to avoid

- Fixed headline lengths, power-word lists, and "always use numbers" rules without context
- Platform tips presented without a source or date
- Lift percentages from vendor case studies with no spend, sample, or method
